> **Where a deliverable has no measurable criteria** — a training curriculum, a validation report, a site survey — acceptance is on delivery of the document itself. The criterion is "delivered and legible," not "approved in substance." A customer who wants approval in substance must say so in the Order, because a deliverable accepted only on subjective approval is a deliverable that is never accepted.

**This determination must be made within 10 Business Days of delivery.** After that period, the deliverable is deemed accepted (D04 SOW-1 §7).

> **Be specific or accept it.** A non-acceptance notice that says "not satisfactory" without naming the failed criterion and the remedy is ineffective: it does not tell us what to fix, and it does not stop the deemed-acceptance clock from running. This protects both sides.

> **Limit on re-review:** a deliverable may be rejected **once** for material non-conformity. A second rejection requires a Change Order (F03) or a formal dispute under D07 §14 — because a cycle of endless rejection is a scope dispute wearing a process costume.

---

## Part 6 — Effect of acceptance

1. **Acceptance discharges the obligation to deliver**, subject to any warranty in D07 §12 and the support obligations in D06.
2. Acceptance does **not** waive any warranty, and does **not** transfer IP (D13).
3. Acceptance of a deliverable does **not** accept a different deliverable, and does not accept future deliverables.
4. Where acceptance triggers a payment milestone, the invoice may be issued on the acceptance date.
