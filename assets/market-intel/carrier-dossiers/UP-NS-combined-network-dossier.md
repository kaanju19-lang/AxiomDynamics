# Union Pacific / Norfolk Southern — Combined Network Dossier

**Why these are one dossier:** Union Pacific is acquiring Norfolk Southern for $85B. Announced July 2025, STB accepted the revised application as complete on 28 May 2026, opponents' dismissal motions denied 22 Sept 2026, expected close H2 2027. The combined network would be 50,000+ route miles across 43 states and ~100 ports.

Treating them as two separate prospects means pitching the same company twice and being caught not knowing. One dossier, two networks, one integration.

## 2. The Sunset Corridor — and why it is not the Southern Transcon

The obvious analogue to BNSF's Southern Transcon, and the comparison mostly holds structurally but **fails on density**:

| | BNSF Southern Transcon | UP Sunset Route |
|---|---|---|
| Length | ~2,200 mi LA–Chicago | 757 mi LA–El Paso, continuing to Houston/New Orleans |
| Traffic | 200+ trains/day | ~28–39 trains/day (Wellton AZ 2024: 28/day) |
| Double track | ~99% after Nov 2024 Emporia cutover | ~80% by 2015; **127 mi of single track remained** — UP resumed work 2024 |
| Subdivisions | 11, across 9 states | Yuma → Gila → Lordsburg → Valentine → Sanderson → Del Rio → Glidden → Houston → Lafayette → Terminal |
| Share | ~70% of BNSF intermodal; ~1/3 of N. American intermodal | Key line to Houston/New Orleans; some volume shifted to Texas & Pacific via Dallas |

**The commercial consequence is direct and unwelcome.** On our site-fee model, a 757-mile corridor at 40–64 mile detector spacing is **11–18 sites** — $450K–$765K/year in platform fees — for a corridor carrying roughly a seventh of the Southern Transcon's traffic (which prices at 34–55 sites and reflects 200+ trains/day).

**The BNSF corridor economics do not transfer to the Sunset Route at the same price point.** If we quote BNSF-shaped numbers to UP we will be wrong by a wide margin, and their network planning team will know it inside five minutes.

Two legitimate responses, and we should decide between them before the meeting:

1. **Lead with the single-division deployment, not the corridor.** A yard or one high-density subdivision prices correctly and proves value without the site-fee blowout.
2. **Price the corridor on axle throughput, and be honest that UP's throughput justifies a smaller license than BNSF's.** A smaller deal that closes beats a large one that fails diligence.

Option 1 is what I'd recommend for UP specifically.

## 3. Norfolk Southern — the remediation case

- **East Palestine, Feb 3, 2023.** 38 cars derailed, 11 carrying hazardous materials, controlled burn of vinyl chloride. Cleanup and settlement in the $1B+ range; the DOJ/EPA consent decree is the reference figure.
- **The specific failure our platform addresses:** a wayside HBD alarmed, the train was moved ~13 miles to a siding, and it derailed en route. NTSB's investigation centred on detector spacing, alarm thresholds, and *response discipline* — not on sensor absence.
- **NS suspended its buyback program** under the merger agreement's pre-closing restrictions, which include capital expenditure limits.

**The NS conversation is different in kind from BNSF's.** BNSF is prevention — a sale. NS is remediation and defensible regulatory posture — the buyer has already paid, already has NTSB recommendations against their response process, and is currently restricted on capex. That is a hard sell today and a strong one in 2028 when the merger closes and the combined entity needs one integration standard.

## 4. The merger overlay — the strategic fact that matters most

**Timeline:**

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| Jul 2025 | Merger announced, $85B |
| 19 Dec 2025 | Original STB application filed |
| 16 Jan 2026 | STB rejects as incomplete (deficient market-share projections, missing merger agreement docs, misclassified TRRA terminal transaction) |
| 30 Apr 2026 | Revised application filed |
| 28 May 2026 | STB **accepts as complete**, but places proceeding and environmental review **in abeyance**; orders supplemental information by 27 Jul 2026. Flags Committed Gateway Pricing, 2-to-1/3-to-2 shipper access, the 2.1M truckload diversion / $3.5B savings projections, arbitration/service assurance, TTX and terminal railroad plans |
| 22 Sep 2026 | STB **denies** BNSF, CSX and shipper motions to dismiss; merits review continues |
| H2 2027 (expected) | Close |

- First merger reviewed under the STB's **2001 Major Merger Rules** — the heavier public-interest burden.
- **Richard Kloster** (board member) filed a concurring opinion calling the application "shallow" and lacking meaningful concessions. He voted not to dismiss but signalled skepticism. Do not assume approval is a formality.
- Pre-closing, NS is contractually restricted on capex, contracts, asset sales, indebtedness, dividends and buybacks.

**What this means for selling:**

- **A large multi-year deployment to either carrier is not buyable right now.** Pre-closing capex restrictions plus STB scrutiny of every commitment makes a novel vendor contract a liability for both sides.
- **A $370K pilot sits underneath that.** It is small, operationally framed, and produces evidence rather than obligation.
- **The window is post-close.** One integration standard across 50,000 miles and 43 states is the single largest addressable deployment in North American rail. Being the platform already validated inside one of the two networks at close is worth more than any discount we could offer.

## 5. BNSF is now the opposing litigant, not a neutral prospect

BNSF petitioned the STB to dismiss the UP–NS application. That reframes the BNSF pitch substantially.

A carrier publicly arguing that its competitor's combination is anti-competitive is under pressure to demonstrate it runs the safer, better-run network on its own. **The pitch to BNSF is not "save money" — it is "be the railroad that proved bearing failures can be caught 18 hours out, while the merger argument is on the front page."**

That is a stronger opening than a cost case, and it is time-limited to the STB proceeding. It also means BNSF's competitive position versus a 50,000-mile transcontinental is the strategic frame their executives are living in for the next eighteen months.

Note the sensitivity: raising the merger in a BNSF room is fine and expected. Raising it carelessly in a UP or NS room — where the deal is still unapproved — is not. Read the room.

## 6. What we do not know

- **UP's operating division structure** is not published in a form we could verify. The five service units are third-party.
- **UP's detector spacing and unit count** — industry norm is 40–64 miles; not UP-specific.
- **Whether the combined entity will standardise on one detector vendor, or rationalise two fleets.** This is a real opportunity and a real risk.
- **Whether a pilot signed with either party pre-close survives integration.** It should, but structure it to make that explicit — assignability and survival clauses matter here in a way they don't with BNSF.
