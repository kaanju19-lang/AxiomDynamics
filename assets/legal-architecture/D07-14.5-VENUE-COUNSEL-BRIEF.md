## 1. The question, stated exactly

D07 §14.5(a) says three things must be decided together: the **governing law**
(the substantive law applied), the **seat or forum** (the courts, or the arbitral
seat and institution), and the **mediation provider** under §14.2.
D07 §14.5(b) says the Supplier decides, with advice of counsel qualified in the
candidate jurisdiction, before this Agreement is first executed with any customer,
and records it in a signed addendum.

**This document is the material that goes to that counsel.** It sets out the
facts that constrain the choice, the options and what each actually buys, and the
specific questions counsel is being asked to answer. Q6 §9 already states the
position this file inherits: the correct venue and governing law is an open
counsel decision, not a formation question.

**This is not legal advice.** Nothing here should be relied on as a statement of
law. It is prepared by a non-lawyer to make a paid hour with a lawyer productive.
Every statutory and procedural statement below carries its verification status;
where a point could not be confirmed against a primary source, it says so rather
than smoothing it over.

## 2. What the docket has already committed to, which limits the answer

A venue clause cannot be drafted in isolation from the instrument it sits in.
Five constraints are already fixed:

**(a) The entity is a Texas LLC.** Q6 recommends Texas formation and §3 of that
document sets out why. The entity's principal place of business is Texas. This
matters twice: it makes a Texas forum the natural seat, and it makes Texas the
default under D07 §14.5(d) if the decision is never completed.

**(b) The contract is a master agreement with Orders, not a single deal.** D07 is
the authoritative statement of terms; D03 incorporates it and §19 expressly
declines to hold its own venue clause, with the reasoning stated: *"where two
instruments each carry their own choice of law, they drift, and the fifth Order
is signed under whichever one nobody checked."* Any decision must therefore live
in exactly one place — D07 §14.5 — and the addendum must amend that section, not
add a parallel clause elsewhere.

**(c) The liability cap is twelve months' fees.** D07 §8.1, which at the pilot is
**$370,000** and at a 2,000-axle production licence is **$1,050,000**. This is the
single most important number for choosing a forum, and §4 explains why.

**(d) The counterparties are Texas-headquartered Class I carriers.** D07 §14.5(c)
records Texas law and a Texas venue as the working expectation on that basis,
explicitly as an expectation and not as a term.

**(e) The fallback in §14.5(d) already exists and is already bad.** If an Order is
executed while §14.5 is open, disputes go to the courts of the Supplier's
principal place of business. That is a functioning but undesigned outcome: it
chooses Texas by accident, and §14.5(d) itself says its use *"is itself evidence
that (b) was not completed."* Completing the decision is what replaces it.

## 3. The threshold problem — this is the finding that matters most

**Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code § 15.020** ("Major Transactions: Specification of
Venue by Agreement") validates a contractual choice of venue only where the
agreement is a **"major transaction"** — one under which a party pays or receives
consideration with an **aggregate stated value equal to or greater than
$1 million**, appearing **on the face of the agreement**.

**The pilot is $370,000.** D02 §3 and D08 §2 both state that figure: $180,000
licence, $65,000 commissioning support, $40,000 training, $35,000 validation,
$50,000 support. That is well below the threshold. *Verified against the statute
summary and the practitioner commentary; the $1M must be stated on the face of the
agreement rather than established later from damages.*

Three consequences, and they point in different directions:

**(i) A bare "venue is Texas" clause does not do the work on a pilot.** If the
statute does not apply, the clause is not given the statutory safe harbour, and a
party resisting it has a statutory argument the drafting did not need to create.
The ordinary-law forum-selection analysis would still apply, but the company would
be litigating the clause instead of litigating the dispute. **This is the kind of
defect nobody finds until it is expensive.**

**(ii) It may apply to production — and that is a drafting question, not a
pricing one.** D08 §3 states $300,000/year at 500 axles and **$1,050,000/year at
2,000 axles.** D08 §4's three-year table gives **$1,180,000** total for a single
yard at 500 axles. Whether a master agreement stating an annual rate and a
three-year term "states" an aggregate value over $1M on its face is precisely a
question for counsel, and the answer determines whether one clause can serve both
the pilot and production or whether the pilot needs a different mechanism.

**(iii) The cleanest answer may not be a Texas-court clause at all.** If venue
selection is unreliable below $1M, the options are to (a) rely on § 15.020 only
where it applies and accept the ordinary-law analysis elsewhere, (b) draft the
clause so the aggregate is stated on the face where the deal supports it, or
(c) use arbitration, which does not depend on § 15.020 at all because it is
enforced under the FAA and the Texas Arbitration Act rather than the venue
statute. **Counsel should be asked which of these is correct, not assumed.**

> **Why this section is the point of the document.** A venue clause that looks
> standard, is signed, and is then not enforced in the one dispute it was written
> for is worse than the open item it replaced — because the open item was honest
> and the clause would not be. F00 Rule 7 draws that distinction for blanks; this
> is the same distinction applied to a clause that is present but toothless.

## 4. The real cost driver: it is not the filing fee

If arbitration is on the table — and it should be, for the reason in §3(iii) —
the commercial analysis that matters is not the administrative filing fee.

**Verified:** the AAA runs two domestic commercial fee schedules, **Standard** and
**Flexible**, and the Flexible schedule roughly halves the initial filing fee but
adds a **Proceed Fee** due within 90 days which is nearly as large, so the discount
largely evaporates if the case stays open; filing and proceed fees under the
Flexible schedule are non-refundable. Commercial filing fees start in the region of
**$1,725** depending on claim amount. For a non-monetary claim the minimum filing
fee applies. Commercial **mediation** carries a **$350** non-refundable filing fee
per party. The AAA's own fee calculator states plainly that **"arbitrator
compensation and expenses are not included in the fee schedule."**

**That last sentence is the whole commercial point.** The filing fee is a few
thousand dollars and is not the risk. The risk is the arbitrator's hourly
compensation multiplied by the hours the case takes, and it is uncapped and
unstated. **The comparison figures found:** JAMS charges a **$2,500** filing fee
for two-party matters and **$4,000** for three or more; CPR charges **$250** for an
individual claimant with a **$1,500** proceed fee for a company respondent.

**For a company with a $370,000 pilot and a cap at that level, three things follow:**

1. **Specify one arbitrator.** A clause silent on the number does not get you one;
   the rules' default for larger disputes is three, and three arbitrators is
   roughly three times the cost of the thing you were trying to make cheaper.
2. **Consider expedited procedures.** The AAA commercial rules contain an expedited
   track below a claim-value threshold. Whether this docket's likely disputes fall
   under it is a counsel question, but the clause should opt in expressly rather
   than rely on the default.
3. **Cost-shifting must be drafted, not assumed.** D07 §14.6 currently says each
   party bears its own costs unless the forum awards costs. In arbitration, the
   forum's power to award costs is set by the rules and the clause. **Do not leave
   §14.6 standing unexamined once a forum is chosen** — it was written when the
   forum was open.

**What could not be verified:** the full claim-amount-by-claim-amount
administrative fee table, and the AAA's stated arbitrator hourly compensation
ranges. Those specific figures were not retrievable from the sources reached.
**They must be pulled from the current schedule at adr.org before any figure is
quoted in a document**, and no number from this paragraph should be copied into a
client-facing paper without that check.

## 5. The options, and what each actually buys

### Option A — Texas law, Texas courts (state), exclusive jurisdiction

**Buys:** the least surprising arrangement for a Texas entity contracting with a
Texas-headquartered carrier (D07 §14.5(c)). Cheap to draft, cheap to administer,
familiar to every procurement department in the target market. Enforceable as a
forum-selection clause under ordinary Texas law, which presumes such clauses valid
and places the burden on the party resisting — *In re AutoNation*, 228 S.W.3d 663
(Tex. 2007) is the anchor. *Verified as the general standard; the case citation
should be confirmed by counsel before it is relied on in a memo of law.*

**Costs:** the § 15.020 threshold problem in §3 applies directly. Also — and this
is the honest part — **Texas courts mean public filings.** A dispute over a
railroad's axle data, a reviewer's disposition, or a platform's performance would
be litigated in a public record, and the confidentiality provisions in D11 do not
control what a court file contains.

### Option B — Texas law, AAA arbitration seated in Texas (Dallas or Houston)

**Buys:** sidesteps § 15.020 entirely, because arbitration is enforced under the
FAA and the Texas Arbitration Act rather than the venue statute. Confidential by
default. One arbitrator and expedited procedures can be specified. The seat choice
still satisfies D07 §14.5(c)'s Texas expectation.

**Costs:** arbitrator compensation, uncapped, as §4 sets out. Loses the
court's default evidentiary and procedural machinery, which for a small company
without trial experience is not nothing. And the clause must actually be drafted —
a silent clause gets the rules' defaults, which are not the cheap ones.

### Option C — Texas law, JAMS arbitration seated in Texas

**Buys:** broadly the same as Option B, with a different administrator and a
different fee posture. JAMS charges more at filing ($2,500 two-party, $4,000
three-plus) but the administrative structure differs. Some counsel prefer JAMS for
complex commercial matters and AAA for volume.

**Costs:** same arbitrator-compensation exposure. The choice between B and C is
properly a question for counsel from experience, not for a comparison table.

### Option D — Delaware law, Delaware courts

**Should be recorded as considered and rejected, not omitted.** Q6 §3 already
rejects Delaware registration on the ground that the Court of Chancery's advantage
is shareholder litigation this company does not have. The same reasoning applies
with more force here: a Delaware choice of law in a contract between a Texas LLC
and a Texas carrier buys nothing, costs an out-of-state forum to travel to, and
makes the clause more contestable rather than less.

### On mediation (D07 §14.2)

Currently `[the mediation rules of the chosen provider]` — a defined-term bracket
under F00 Rule 4, correctly not a blank. It must be settled in the same decision.
The commercial mediation fee is modest (AAA mediation: **$350** non-refundable
filing fee per party, verified). **The important drafting point is sequencing:**
§14.2 already requires mediation for thirty days *before* proceedings. Confirm with
counsel that a mediation condition precedent is enforceable in the chosen forum and
does not accidentally operate as a limitation-period trap.

## 6. The recommendation, labelled as one

**The recommendation of this brief is Option B: Texas law, AAA Commercial
Arbitration, seated in Dallas or Houston, one arbitrator, expedited procedures
elected, and costs to follow the award under a redrafted §14.6.**

The reasoning, in the order it actually matters:

1. **It is the only option that does not depend on the § 15.020 threshold being
   satisfied.** The pilot is $370,000 and will be the first contract signed. The
   first contract is exactly the one whose dispute clause must work.
2. **Confidentiality is a substantive requirement of this business, not a
   preference.** The docket's whole posture is that the Platform produces
   information a Railroad acts on. A public dispute over that information is a
   reputational event independent of its merits, and D11 alone does not prevent it.
3. **It keeps the Texas expectation in §14.5(c) intact** — Texas law, Texas seat —
   so the clause does not contradict the reasoning already written into the
   document and shown to counterparties.
4. **The cost objection is real and is addressed by drafting, not by choosing a
   different forum.** One arbitrator plus expedited procedures plus a drafted cost
   provision deals with most of it.

**What would change this recommendation:** if counsel advises that a Class I
carrier's procurement function will not accept arbitration as a matter of policy,
then the practical answer is Option A plus a deliberate second look at how the
aggregate is stated on the face of production Orders. **That is a market fact, not
a legal one, and it is the strongest argument against the recommendation in this
document.** It should be asked of counsel and, separately, of anyone who has been
through a Class I procurement cycle.

## 7. The questions actually being put to counsel

1. Is the § 15.020 analysis in §3 correct — that a $370,000 agreement is not a
   "major transaction," and that a bare venue clause in it therefore gets no
   statutory safe harbour?
2. Does a master agreement stating an **annual** rate of $1,050,000 and a multi-year
   term state an "aggregate value" on its face for § 15.020 purposes, or must the
   aggregate be written out?
3. Given (1) and (2), is **AAA arbitration seated in Texas** the recommended
   structure for an entity of this size and this counterparty profile?
4. If arbitration: one arbitrator, expedited procedures, and what cost provision —
   and does D07 §14.6 need redrafting to make it operative?
5. Is the thirty-day mediation condition in §14.2 enforceable, and does it create
   any limitation-period risk?
6. Which mediation provider, and does the choice interact with the arbitral
   institution?
7. Does anything in D12 (regulatory status) or D09 (data processing) create a
   forum constraint that overrides the commercial preference — for example an
   obligation to litigate a data-protection claim somewhere specific?

**Question 7 is the one most likely to be missed.** It is included because the
answer may be a constraint rather than a preference, and a constraint discovered
after execution is the worst kind.

## 8. What happens after counsel answers

The decision is recorded in a **signed addendum to D07**, not by editing §14.5 in
place, because §14.5(b) already says so and because the addendum is the record
that the decision was made by the right people with advice. The mechanical steps:

1. Addendum drafted from the answer to §7, signed by the Supplier's authorised
   signatory, retained under F00 Rule 5.
2. D07 §14.5 rewritten from an open item to a completed term, **with the date and
   the addendum reference stated**. Per F00 Rule 7, the change from "unmade
   decision" to "decision" is itself a fact the document must record.
3. D07 §14.2's provider bracket resolved.
4. D07 §14.6 examined against the chosen forum.
5. D03 §19 needs no change — it delegates and is correct — but it should be read
   once afterwards to confirm the delegation still resolves.
6. D01 Part 4's final gate already requires "Counsel has reviewed D02, D03, D07,
   D09, D12, D13, D14." The §14.5 addendum is the evidence that this specific
   entry in that gate is satisfied, rather than an assumption that it is.

## 9. What this brief does not establish

Recorded here because a brief that claims more than it verified is the failure
mode this project keeps finding.

- **The § 15.020 analysis is from statute summaries and practitioner commentary,
  not from the statute text.** The Texas statutes site was not reachable from this
  environment. **Counsel should read §§ 15.018–15.020 directly.**
- **The Texas construction-contract legislation (Bus. & Com. Code ch. 272, which
  voids out-of-state forum and choice-of-law clauses) was checked and does not
  apply to this contract** — it is limited to construction contracts concerning
  Texas real property. It was checked because a search result surfaced it as a
  recent Texas trend, and a reader who saw that headline and assumed it applied
  would have reached the wrong conclusion. **Sources conflicted on the effective
  date (2023 amendment versus 2025 legislation); it does not matter here, because
  the contract is not a construction contract, but it should not be quoted.**
- **The AAA and JAMS fee figures are from summaries and the fee calculator, not
  from the current schedules in full.** Pull the current PDF before quoting any
  number publicly.
- **The *In re AutoNation* citation** should be verified by counsel. It was
  identified from search results, not read.
- **No Texas-local-counsel opinion has been obtained.** That is the whole point of
  this document existing.
