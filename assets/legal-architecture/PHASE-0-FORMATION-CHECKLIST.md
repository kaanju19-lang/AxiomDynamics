`D07-14.5-VENUE-COUNSEL-BRIEF.md`

**Prepared:** 2026-11-11 (chat-5)

---

## 1. What this file is, and what it deliberately is not

Q6 already decides entity form (**Texas LLC**), state (**Texas**), and the
conversion trigger (**institutional round, option pool, or acquisition approach**).
It also states the thing most formation checklists bury: **formation is not the main
protection and should not be treated as though it were** — the largest exposure is a
personal guarantee, and no amount of correct formation survives one.

This file does not re-argue any of that. **It is the action list**, with the
dependencies ordered so that the cheapest correct thing happens first and the item
that actually blocks a customer signature is visible rather than buried.

**Nothing here is a decision.** Where a decision is needed it says so and names
who must make it, per F00 Rule 7.

---

## 2. The gate, stated honestly

D01 Part 4 says every line must be complete before the first document is signed by
a customer. That is the right standard, and it is worth being blunt about where the
project actually stands against it: **almost none of the entity items are done.**
The docket is built; the company is not.

That is not a criticism — building 37 docket documents before forming an entity is
a defensible order of operations, because the entity is cheap and the documents
were the hard part. But it means **the critical path to a first customer
conversation is now this file**, not the code and not the docket.

## 3. Track A — The entity itself

Nothing downstream can be done without the first two lines. Everything else waits.

| # | Action | Cost | Depends on | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | File **Certificate of Formation (Form 205)** with Texas Secretary of State | **$300** one-time | Nothing | ☐ Not done |
| A2 | Obtain **EIN** from IRS (free, online) | $0 | A1 | ☐ Not done |
| A3 | **Operating agreement** — drafted with counsel | Counsel time | A1 | ☐ Not done |
| A4 | Open **business bank account**; no commingling from day one | $0 to open | A1, A2 | ☐ Not done |
| A5 | **Registered agent** — service or self | ~$50–$300/yr | A1 | ☐ Not done |
| A6 | Optional: **name reservation** before filing | $40 | Nothing | ☐ Optional |
| A7 | Diary the **Public Information Report**, due **15 May** annually | $0 | A1 | ☐ Diary it |
| A8 | Pull **good standing certificate** before first signature | Small fee | A1, A7 | ☐ Not done |

**Verified costs and the traps** (from Q6 §3, checked against official sources):

- **$300** is the Texas LLC Certificate of Formation fee — the cheapest part of
  forming a company and the least important.
- **Franchise tax is $0** below the **$2,650,000** annualized total revenue
  threshold for 2026. **The filing obligation does not disappear because the tax
  is zero.** A7 exists because treating "no tax due" as "no filing" is the most
  common way a small company silently loses good standing.
- **A3 is where the money should go.** Q6 §4 is explicit: *"The single highest-value
  spend in the formation process is a proper operating agreement reviewed by
  counsel, not the filing fee."* A3 is the only line on this table where paying
  more buys materially more protection.
- **A7 has a first-five-years wrinkle.** A new entity is not required to file a No
  Tax Due Report in its first five years, but the Public Information Report is
  still due. Do not read the five-year relief as covering A7.

## 4. Track B — The things the docket promises that only exist if bound

D03 §9 states insurance limits and D07 §10 states a contractual E&O limit. **Those
figures must match the policies actually bound, and today no policy exists.**
D03 §9 carries the honest note about this: **$5,000,000 E&O is not purchasable by a
pre-revenue company at a premium the business can carry**, and a certificate that
does not match the contract is discovered at the worst possible moment.

| # | Action | Note | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | Broker conversation: **CGL** ($1M/occurrence, $2M aggregate per D03 §9) | Quotable pre-revenue | ☐ |
| B2 | **Technology E&O / Professional Liability** — bind at **$2,000,000** | D07 §10.1 commits to step up to $5M at revenue >$1M or first production licence. **Do not quote $5M as held today.** | ☐ |
| B3 | **Cyber liability** — $1,000,000 per D03 §9 | | ☐ |
| B4 | **Workers' Comp / Employer's Liability** | Statutory where required; $1M EL per D03 §9 | ☐ |
| B5 | Reconcile bound limits against **D03 §9 and D07 §10** and amend whichever is wrong | D01 Part 4 has a checkbox for exactly this | ☐ |
| B6 | Keep **certificates of insurance** available on request | D03 §9 | ☐ |

**B2 is the one to get right.** The docket's E&O story is: bind $2M now, commit
contractually to step up to the highest commercially available limit up to $5M once
revenue exceeds $1M. That is an honest structure. **Quoting $5M as current coverage
would be a misrepresentation that a certificate would expose.** D03 §9 and D07 §10
must always agree with the binder, and B5 exists because they will drift.

## 5. Track C — The item that actually blocks first execution

**D07 §14.5 — governing law, seat and forum.** This is not a formation item and
forming the entity does not answer it. It is a **counsel decision**, and D07
§14.5(b) requires it to be recorded in a signed addendum **before this Agreement is
first executed with any customer.**

The brief is prepared: `D07-14.5-VENUE-COUNSEL-BRIEF.md`. It sets out the options,
recommends AAA arbitration seated in Texas with reasons, and lists the seven
questions to put to counsel. **The § 15.020 "$1 million on the face" threshold
finding is the reason this cannot be left to a standard clause.**

| # | Action | Status |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | Engage Texas counsel (commercial contracts) | ☐ |
| C2 | Put the seven questions in the brief's §7 to counsel | ☐ Prepared, not asked |
| C3 | Addendum drafted, signed, retained per F00 Rule 5 | ☐ |
| C4 | D07 §14.5 rewritten from open item to completed term, with date and addendum reference | ☐ |
| C5 | D07 §14.2 provider bracket resolved; §14.6 re-examined against the chosen forum | ☐ |
| C6 | D03 §19 read once to confirm the delegation still resolves (no change expected) | ☐ |

**C1 and A3 should be the same engagement if possible.** Counsel reviewing the
operating agreement and counsel answering the §14.5 questions is one retainer, not
two, and the §14.5 decision benefits from someone who has read the agreement.

## 7. Track E — The guarantee, which is not a formation item but erases one

Q6 §4 is the most important page in the formation analysis and belongs on this
list even though it is not a filing.

**A Class I carrier's procurement function will, on a vendor with no balance sheet,
ask the principal to guarantee obligations personally.** A guarantee converts the
LLC into personal exposure for exactly the obligations that matter most, and no
formation step survives it.

| # | Action | Status |
|---|---|---|
| E1 | Pre-decide the **refusal position** on a payment guarantee: offer pilot credit, performance bond, escrow, or staged payment instead | ☐ |
| E2 | Pre-decide that an **indemnity guarantee for third-party injury** is never signed without counsel, and never as an Order schedule | ☐ |
| E3 | **Whoever signs must know they are signing personally** — state this in writing before any guarantee discussion | ☐ |

**E1–E3 cost nothing and are worth more than the $300 filing.** They are listed
last because they happen last chronologically — but they are the highest-value lines
on this page. Q6 §4 puts it plainly: *"A signature on a guarantee is the single most
consequential document this company will sign, and it is usually signed by someone
who thinks of it as paperwork."*

## 9. What this checklist does not decide

- **Who signs** for the entity, and whether authority is documented. D01 Part 4
  requires it; the operating agreement grants it; this file does not assume it.
- **The operating agreement's terms** — vesting, transfer restrictions, deadlock,
  member departures. Q6 §6 defers these to counsel.
- **Whether the founder holds IP personally or assigns it.** Q6 §6 and D13 §9
  interact here and the operating agreement must not contradict D13.
- **The §14.5 decision itself.** That is Track C, and it is counsel's.
- **Whether to convert to a Delaware C-corporation.** Q6 §5 sets the triggers; none
  has fired.
